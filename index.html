We don't drop in a pump and leave.

Amani Water Project works alongside communities in Kenya, Uganda, and Ethiopia to build wells that last for decades, not seasons. Every project starts with a hydrogeological survey, includes a trained local water committee, and comes with a maintenance fund the community controls.

Amina Wanjiru, a mother of four in Turkana County, used to leave home before sunrise to reach the nearest water point. Since her village's well opened in 2023, she runs a small vegetable garden with the time she used to spend walking.

"My daughters go to school now instead of carrying water. That's the whole difference."

From dry ground to running water

01

Survey the site

Local hydrogeologists identify a reliable groundwater source and confirm it won't run dry within the decade.

02

Drill and build

A local drilling crew installs the well and hand pump, hiring workers from the community wherever possible.

03

Train and hand over

A community water committee is trained on upkeep and given a repair fund, so the well stays running for good.
